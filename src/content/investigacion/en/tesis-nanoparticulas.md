Undergraduate thesis for the degree of Biomedical Engineer, supervised by PhD
Christian Quijia. It addresses antimicrobial resistance (AMR) from the
materials side: bimetallic silver nanocomposites synthesized by a green route
and anchored to cotton fibers, aimed at hospital textiles such as gowns,
bedding and curtains.

## Synthesis

*Aloe vera* extract is used as the reducing agent in the green synthesis of
three nanocomposite systems: **Ag-Cu, Ag-Mg and Ag-Zn**.

## Characterization

The structure and functionality of the nanocomposites were examined with a
complementary set of techniques:

- **UV-Vis**: a bathochromic shift in the absorption peaks as Cu content rises
  in the Ag-Cu systems, indicating plasmonic coupling and stronger electronic
  interactions.
- **XRD**: crystalline phases and crystallite sizes, which determine
  antimicrobial efficacy.
- **FTIR**: confirmed metal-oxygen bonds.
- **Raman**: clarified the surface chemistry and the stabilizing role of the
  organic residues from *Aloe vera*.
- **TGA**: thermal behavior.
- **SEM-EDX**: confirmed the anchoring of the nanoparticles to the cellulose
  matrix, probably through electrostatic interactions and hydrogen bonds.

## Results

Impregnation preserves the structural integrity of the fabric and strengthens
the localized antimicrobial effect. The Ag-Cu and Ag-Zn systems stayed retained
in the cotton fibers throughout the process.

Activity was tested against *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Escherichia coli* using
minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) and disk diffusion assays. **The Ag-Cu
nanocomposite at a 3:1 molar ratio showed the highest antimicrobial activity**,
with clear inhibition zones and the lowest MIC values among the systems tested,
even at reduced concentrations.
